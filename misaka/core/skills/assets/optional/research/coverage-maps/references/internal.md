# Internal maps: how disciplines sort their own literature

The maps in `disciplines.md` are drawn by statisticians and funders. These are drawn by
the indexers of each field's own bibliography, so each one shows what that field counts as
a topic, a period, a genre, an approach — and, in its silences, what it does not count.
Scan the one for each discipline named in the plan, and the one for the neighbouring
discipline that would frame the question differently.

Transcribed 2026-08-25, two levels deep unless the second level is enormous (then the top
level and a count). Where a scheme is only visible inside a subscription, the publisher's
public description is given and the rest is marked (unverified). Names follow the source's
own language and script; glosses in parentheses. Schemes kept elsewhere: OCM and JEL in
`traditions.md`, Iconclass in `facets.md`.

## 2. MLA International Bibliography (languages, literatures, folklore, film, rhetoric)

Source: https://www.mla.org/Publications/MLA-International-Bibliography/About-the-MLA-International-Bibliography ;
https://www.ebsco.com/products/research-databases/mla-international-bibliography ;
field definitions at https://support.ebsco.com/help/index.php?help_id=DB%3A1075&int=ehost&lang=en
(accessed 2026-08-25)

The MLA Thesaurus (675,000+ subject terms, names and works) is visible only inside the
subscription, so the scheme is given as the publisher's scope statement plus the record
structure, which is the real map.

- Scope: world languages and literatures, linguistics, dramatic arts, film, folklore,
  rhetoric and writing studies, teaching of language, history of publishing; 3 million+
  records from the late 19th century on, 70+ languages, about 70,000 new records a year.
- The "General Subject Areas" every record carries: Subject Literature (a national
  literature, e.g. Palestinian literature) · Subject Language · Period (e.g. 1900-1999,
  Victorian period) · Genre and Genre/Classification (e.g. drama; folk literature) ·
  Primary Subject Author · Primary Subject Work · Literary Theme/Motif · Literature Topic ·
  Linguistics Topic · Folklore Topic · Media (e.g. theater) · Location · Group (e.g. Christian).
- Further subject fields: Literary Feature · Literary Technique · Literary Process (e.g.
  translation) · Literary Source and Literary Influence (which literature fed which) ·
  Scholarly Approach (e.g. linguistic approach) · Scholarly Theory or Discipline (e.g.
  feminism) · Scholarly Tool or Device · Alternate Language of Literary Work.

So an MLA record is national literature × period × genre × author or work × theme or
technique × approach; the printed volumes were arranged by national literature, then
period, then author (from memory, unverified). A plan that names a literature and a period
but no genre, approach, or source-and-influence has left three MLA axes empty.

## 3. Historical Abstracts and America: History and Life (EBSCO)

Source: https://www.ebsco.com/products/research-databases/historical-abstracts ;
https://www.ebsco.com/products/research-databases/america-history-and-life ;
https://about.ebsco.com/products/research-databases/historical-abstracts-full-text
(accessed 2026-08-25)

- Historical Abstracts: "world history (excluding the United States and Canada) from the
  15th century to the present"; 2,210 active indexed journals (2,058 peer-reviewed). The
  full-text edition's page says "from ancient times to the present"; take 1450 as the
  historical cut-off and treat earlier coverage as incidental.
- America: History and Life: "history and culture of the United States and Canada from
  prehistory to the present"; 1,296 active indexed journals.
- The split between the two is itself a map: US and Canada versus everyone else, and, for
  everyone else, post-1450 only — pre-1450 Europe, Asia and Africa belong to neither and
  need L'Année philologique, the International Medieval Bibliography, or an area bibliography.
- Indexing: EBSCO subject headings plus a Historical Period field and limiter (search by
  date range, e.g. 1558-1603) — the distinctive access point.
- The old ABC-Clio subject classification of the printed volumes is not published on the
  public pages (unverified); inside the database browse the Subject and Historical Period
  indexes rather than looking for a classification tree.

## 4. Linguistic Bibliography / Bibliographie Linguistique (Brill)

Source: https://bibliographies.brillonline.com/browse/linguistic-bibliography (accessed
2026-08-25); print structure from P. Swiggers's review of the 2017–2019 volumes,
https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/f80c0db8-027b-41da-994d-93e8b30c3c2c (secondary)

- Scope (publisher): all languages and language families; theoretical linguistics;
  biographical data on linguists. About 910,000 records; 1,000+ subject keywords and 4,000
  language keywords (data from 2004 on). Explicitly not prioritised: applied linguistics,
  literary studies, philology, codicology, pedagogy, translation studies — a philological or
  translation question needs another bibliography.
- Classification of the print yearbook, 18 top-level sections: General works · General
  linguistics and related disciplines · Interrelationships between language families ·
  Indo-European languages · Asianic and Mediterranean languages · Basque and the ancient
  languages of the Iberian peninsula · Afro-Asiatic (Hamito-Semitic) languages · Caucasian
  languages · Eurasiatic languages · Burushaski · Dravidian languages · Languages of Mainland
  Southeast Asia · Austronesian, Papuan and Australian languages · Languages of Sub-Saharan
  Africa · Indigenous languages of the Americas · Pidgins and Creoles · Sign languages ·
  Planned languages. Three indices: names, languages, subjects.
- The section names are a bibliographer's convenience, not genetic claims; which section
  holds Sinitic could not be checked from the public pages (unverified) — look before
  assuming.

## 7. Anthropology: the AAA's four fields

Source: https://www.americananthro.org/learn-teach/what-is-anthropology/ (accessed 2026-08-25)

The American Anthropological Association: "these four fields—human biology, archaeology,
cultural anthropology, and linguistics—are understood to be the pillars on which the whole
discipline rests" (elsewhere called biological, archaeological, sociocultural and
linguistic anthropology). Its own worked example, food, shows the four cuts: what is eaten
and what it does to bodies; how it was got in the past; how it is prepared, shared and made
meaningful now; how it is talked about. The AnthroSource (Wiley) subject browse could not
be read through its bot wall (unverified). The Outline of Cultural Materials is in
`traditions.md`.

## 8. Religious studies: Atla Religion Database

Source: https://www.atla.com/research-tool/atla-religion-database/ (accessed 2026-08-25)

Coverage areas as Atla states them, one level: Bible, archaeology, and antiquities · human
culture and society · world religions and religious studies · church history, missions, and
ecumenism · pastoral ministry · theology, philosophy, and ethics. Indexed with Atla subject
headings by specialists working in forty languages. The database descends from Religion
Index One (periodicals), Religion Index Two (multi-author works) and the Index to Book
Reviews in Religion (from memory, unverified). The order of the list is the map: a
Christian theological library's, with "world religions" one area among six. For Buddhist,
Daoist or Islamic material pair it with Index Islamicus or the relevant area bibliography.

## 9. Classics: L'Année philologique

Source: https://about.brepolis.net/lannee-philologique/ (accessed 2026-08-25); the
classification transcribed from the "Table des divisions" of vol. 84 (the volume for
2013), PDF at http://www.ugr.es/~odiseo/PDF/APh_84_red.pdf (the Spanish office, REAPh)

Scope: Greek and Latin literature and linguistics (including early Christian texts and
patristics), Greek and Roman history, art, archaeology, philosophy, religion, mythology,
music, science, and numismatics, papyrology, epigraphy; 1,490,000+ records, 1,000+
journals, abstracts in five languages.

Première partie: Auteurs et textes (ancient authors and texts, alphabetical; epigraphic and
papyrological "texts" such as the carmina epigraphica are filed here too).
Deuxième partie: Matières et disciplines —

- I Littérature: A Généralités, histoire littéraire · B Théories et analyses littéraires
  (approches critiques; poétique des textes; rhétorique; motifs et thèmes) · C Genres
  littéraires (poésie; théâtre; prose narrative et historiographie; art oratoire; prose
  philosophique, scientifique et technique; autres genres)
- II Linguistique: A Généralités, linguistique comparée · B Mycénien · C Grec · D Latin
  (each language: history; phonetics and writing; grammar; lexicology and etymology;
  semantics and pragmatics; stylistics; sociolinguistics and dialectology; translation) ·
  E Langues diverses · F Métrique · G Onomastique
- III Transmission des textes: A Catalogues de manuscrits · B Paléographie, codicologie,
  histoire du livre manuscrit et des bibliothèques · C Histoire des textes, critique
  textuelle et technique de l'édition
- IV Sources non littéraires: A Archéologie (generalities; methods; museum, collection and
  exhibition catalogues; sites; cultural areas by period) · B Épigraphie (Aegean; Greek;
  Italic and Etruscan; Latin; Christian; peripheral) · C Numismatique · D Papyrologie
- V Histoire et civilisation: A Histoire générale · B Vie publique, institutions ·
  C Économie et société · D Histoire régionale et géographie historique · E Mentalités et
  mœurs, cadre matériel et formes de la vie quotidienne · F Religions (comparative; Greek;
  Rome and its empire; Christian — church institutions and history, theology and dogma, cult
  and religious life; others) · G Culture (intellectual life; history of art by area — Aegean,
  Greek, Italic and Etruscan, Roman, late-antique and Christian, peripheral; music, dance and
  spectacle). A, B, C and E are each cut by the same periods: the Aegean world of the 2nd
  millennium to the 12th c. BC; pre-Roman Italy and early Rome, Magna Graecia and Sicily;
  the Greek world from the Dark Ages to Actium; Rome from the Republic to the death of
  Severus Alexander; late antiquity; other peoples.
- VI Droit et justice: Généralités · Droit grec · Droit romain · Droits divers
- VII Philosophie et histoire des idées
- VIII Sciences et techniques: Généralités · Mathématiques · Sciences et techniques
  physiques (astronomy, astrology and time-reckoning; physics, chemistry, alchemy;
  metrology) · de la terre (geology and meteorology; geography, cartography, surveying;
  travel, exploration, navigation and transport; mines) · de la vie (biology, zoology,
  botany, ecology; agriculture; medicine) · Technologie (industry and crafts; building;
  civil engineering; military art; other techniques)
- IX Les études classiques: histoire et tradition des études · documentation et recherche
- X Mélanges et recueils: colloquia; Festschriften; collected papers; personalia

Indices: rubriques collectives; nomina antiqua; geographicus; nomina recentiora; modern
authors. Note that religion, law and science are top-level here, where most literary
bibliographies bury them.

## 10. Art history: BHA / RILA and the International Bibliography of Art

Source: https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/bha/ and https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/index.html
(accessed 2026-08-25)

Getty gives free access to the Bibliography of the History of Art (BHA, from 1990, a merger
of RILA and the Répertoire d'art et d'archéologie) and RILA (1975–1989): "European and
American visual arts material published between 1975 and 2007", 1,200+ journals. OpenBibArt
(Getty, INHA, Inist-CNRS) extends the run to 1910–2007, "arts from late antiquity to the
present day", about 1.2 million records. For material after 2007 the International
Bibliography of Art (IBA, ProQuest) continues the scope; its subject list could not be read
through its bot wall (unverified). The scope statement is the map: European and American,
late antiquity onward — pre-Christian, Asian, African and Islamic art are outside it and
need the area bibliographies. Iconclass, for the subject of images, is in `facets.md`.
