# Learned societies' section lists

A society's sections, forums, divisions or program units are a field's own, most current
self-description: what its members have organised themselves to discuss. Scan them as
`disciplines.md` is scanned, for a dimension the plan forgot, never as a checklist. Names keep
each society's own wording (Chinese names in Chinese); lists longer than about thirty items are
compressed into comma- or semicolon-separated runs. Anything not read from the society's own site
on the access date is marked (unverified). Section lists change yearly; check the source when a
name matters.

## Linguistic Society of America / Linguistic Bibliography — subject map
Source: https://bibliographies.brill.com/LBO/about/subject_keywords (accessed 2026-08-25); LSA pages checked: https://www.lsadc.org/abstracts and https://www.lsadc.org/content.asp?contentid=326 (accessed 2026-08-25)

The LSA has no sections, and no "subfields of linguistics" page was found on lsadc.org: its
careers page names only psycholinguistics, sociolinguistics, historical and computational
linguistics as specializations, and its abstract call asks for a "primary field" chosen inside
the submission system. The Linguistic Bibliography Online (Brill; "last updated August 2026")
publishes no classification tree either; it exposes a subject-keyword thesaurus of about 1,400
terms, with separate lists of languages, geographical keywords, theories/frameworks and
publication types. The systematic headings of the printed Linguistic Bibliography survive in the
thesaurus as compound keywords, which together form the bibliography's own subject map:
Phonetics and phonology; Suprasegmental phonology (prosody); Morphology and word-formation;

text grammar; Lexicon (lexicology and lexicography); Onomastics; Stylistics; Sociolinguistics
and dialectology; Sociolinguistics, language variation; Psycholinguistics and neurolinguistics;
Neurolinguistics and language disorders; Language acquisition (first language acquisition,
child language; second language (acquisition), guided and unguided); Multilingualism, language
contact; Language policy and language planning (status, corpus and acquisition planning;
standardization); Historical linguistics and language change; Comparative linguistics;
Reconstruction; Etymology; Linguistic typology, universals of language; Mathematical and
computational linguistics; Statistical and quantitative linguistics; Corpus linguistics;
Applied linguistics; Translation; Machine translation; Language documentation; Script,
orthography; Writing; Literacy; History of linguistics (Antiquity, Middle Ages, Renaissance,
seventeenth to twenty-first century; Western, Non-Western, Arab, Indian and East Asian
traditions); and the newer fields Biolinguistics, Cognitive linguistics, Cultural linguistics,
Ecolinguistics, Ethnolinguistics, Forensic linguistics, Grapholinguistics, Interlinguistics,
Missionary linguistics, Sociophonetics.
The thesaurus's broader-term hierarchy is shallow: 40 root terms carry narrower terms, the
largest being Generative grammar (23), Consonant (20), Function word (11), Sound change (10),
Semantic role (7), Lexical field (6), Part of speech, Vowel and Aspect (5 each). The numbered
classification of the print volumes, general linguistics followed by language families
(unverified).

## Philosophy — PhilPapers categories and PhilJobs areas
Source: https://philpapers.org/browse/all and https://philjobs.org/ (search form, explained at https://philjobs.org/job/help) (accessed 2026-08-25)

PhilPapers Bibliography: 6,155 categories under seven top-level areas. Second level in full;
third level, in parentheses, where it adds a dimension.
- Metaphysics and Epistemology: Epistemology (epistemological sources; epistemological states and
  properties; epistemological theories; epistemology of specific domains; epistemic normativity;
  formal epistemology; social epistemology; knowledge; skepticism); Metaphilosophy
  (philosophical methods; experimental philosophy); Metaphysics (causation, modality, objects,
  ontology, persons, properties, realism and anti-realism, time, interlevel metaphysics,
  metaontology); Philosophy of Action (free will, decision theory, practical reason); Philosophy
  of Language (meaning, reference, pragmatics, syntax, truth, vagueness and indeterminacy,
  languages, language and society); Philosophy of Mind (consciousness, intentionality,
  perception, metaphysics of mind, mental states and processes); Philosophy of Religion
  (arguments for and against theism, divine attributes, epistemology of religion, specific
  religions); M&E, Misc.
- Value Theory: Aesthetics (aesthetic value, art and artworks, philosophy of film, music,
  literature and visual art, history of aesthetics); Applied Ethics (biomedical, business,
  environmental, professional, political, social and technology ethics); Meta-Ethics (moral
  judgment, realism, epistemology, language, normativity, responsibility, reasoning and
  motivation); Normative Ethics (consequentialism, deontological theories, virtue ethics, moral
  psychology, moral phenomena); Philosophy of Gender, Race, and Sexuality (feminist philosophy;
  philosophy of gender, race, sexual orientation, sexuality); Philosophy of Law (nature of law,
  legal reasoning, authority and obligation, areas of law, law and language); Social and
  Political Philosophy (culture, equality, freedom, government and democracy, international
  philosophy, justice, political theory and views, rights, states and nations, war and violence,
  social philosophy); Value Theory, Miscellaneous.
- Science, Logic, and Mathematics: Logic and Philosophy of Logic; Philosophy of Biology
  (developmental, ecology and conservation, evolutionary, genetics, systematics, teleology and
  function); Philosophy of Cognitive Science (AI, psychology, neuroscience, linguistics,
  psychiatry); Philosophy of Computing and Information (computer ethics, simulation, information,
  virtual reality, philosophy of technology); Philosophy of Mathematics; Philosophy of Physical
  Science (chemistry, cosmology, earth sciences, quantum mechanics, space and time, complex
  systems); Philosophy of Social Science (anthropology, archaeology, economics, education,
  geography, history, law, political science, sociology); Philosophy of Probability (chance,
  interpretations, statistics, decision theory); General Philosophy of Science (explanation, laws

  Hellenistic and Later Ancient; Plato; Aristotle); Medieval and Renaissance (pre-1000; 11th/12th;
