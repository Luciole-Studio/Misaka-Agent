# Compare

A comparison is not summaries side by side. Say **at what level** the two materials (or two lines
of reading) can be compared, at which step they part, and whether the divergence comes from the
material, the concepts or the question itself.

## What to read

1. First read each one accurately on its own: its central question, its key concepts and its
   conclusion. Do not look for likenesses yet.
2. Find a real point of correspondence: two statements that give different answers to the same
   question. It may be a concept, an argument or an example.
3. Find the **earliest place** of divergence: not where the conclusions differ, but the step from
   which they differ. Different premises? Different objects? The same word meaning different
   things in the two places?
4. Check what cannot be compared. If the two are not answering the same question at all, say so
   rather than forcing them together.

## What to write

- **Shared structure**: the question, situation or shape of argument they share.
- **Correspondence of concepts**: where their terms correspond and where they slip. Point out a
  word that looks the same but means something different.
- **Where the divergence starts**: a specific step, with places cited on both sides.
- **Keep the disagreement**: a comparison need not produce one explanation. If both readings stand,
  give the conditions under which each holds.
- **Questions to go on with**: what is worth rereading or asking after the comparison.

## Take care

- Giving each side the same length is not fairness; the weight belongs where they meet, not in two
  summaries.
- Do not erase differences for the sake of unity, and do not manufacture oppositions to seem deep.
- Cite lines of reading by node id, and the text by material and pages or section.
