# Reread

Rereading is not copying out the old notes again. Its value is that later reading has changed your
question, so the same passage shows a different construction. Say clearly **where the change
happened**.

## What to read

1. First read your earlier understanding of the material: the judgements and questions you had
   then.
2. Reread the material itself with your present concerns, not only the old notes. Pay particular
   attention to:
   - passages you passed over then and now find important;
   - what you then read as argument and now see as description or rhetoric;
   - what then looked contradictory, and whether it now has another explanation.
3. Find **the reading that caused the change**: which new line or which concept made this material
   look different.

## What to write

- **Earlier understanding**: the judgements and questions you had, restated briefly and without
  embellishment.
- **What the rereading sees**: what you actually read this time, with places cited.
- **Why it changed**: new material, a new concept, or a changed question.
- **What is still unclear**: what remains vague after rereading, and why.
- **What becomes of the earlier judgement**: revised, kept, or kept alongside the new one. When
  both stand, give the conditions under which each holds.

## Take care

- Do not declare the earlier understanding "wrong" because you reread. First say why it held at
  the time.
- Do not cover the text with later theory. The text must still be readable through your account.
- If the rereading brought no real change, write plainly that it confirmed the earlier
  understanding; do not invent a turn.
